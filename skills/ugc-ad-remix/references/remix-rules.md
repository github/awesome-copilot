# Rebuilding the ad for the user's product

## Keep

- **The hook type.** A problem hook stays a problem hook, about the user's product's problem.
- **The segment order.** If the reference goes hook → problem → proof → offer → cta, so does the remix.
- **The transferable moves** you listed in the breakdown.
- **The pace.** Roughly the same number of shots and total length.

## Change

Every line. The remix is about the user's product, its buyer and its use case. Do not reuse the reference ad's sentences, even with the product name swapped in.

## Facts: only what you were given

Use a product fact only if it appears on the user's product page or the user told you. That includes features, materials, sizes, prices, shipping, guarantees and results.

Never carry over from the reference ad:

- its price, discount, bundle, coupon or "this week only";
- its numbers ("6 hours", "40,000 customers", "3x faster");
- its brand or product names, or its creator's name;
- its claims about what its own product does.

**When a shot needs proof you do not have**, write what the camera shows instead of a number: "Pours hot coffee, waits, sips on camera and nods" rather than "Still hot after 6 hours". If a number would make the shot much stronger, add it to the user's checklist as something to measure.

**When the user has no offer**, end with a plain call to action ("link in bio", "tap the link") instead of inventing one.

Mark every assumption in the output, for example `[assumed: ships in 2 days — confirm]`.

## Claims you never write

- Medical or health outcomes ("cures", "treats", "heals", "clinically proven" without a source from the user).
- Financial results ("make $500 a day").
- Guarantees the user did not state ("works for everyone", "or your money back").
- Comparisons that name a competitor.

## Shot rules

- 4–6 shots, each 4–15 seconds, total 15–30 seconds.
- Each shot has one job that matches its segment role.
- On-screen text is short (under eight words) and readable with sound off.
- The voiceover is something a real person would say: first person, plain words, no slogans.
- The first shot must work in the first two seconds without sound.

## Two versions

Make the two versions differ in something that is worth testing, not just wording. Good pairs: same structure with a different hook type; same hook with a different proof shot; talking-to-camera versus voiceover over product shots.
