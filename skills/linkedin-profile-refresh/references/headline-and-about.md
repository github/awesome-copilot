# Headline and About

## Facts first

- Every claim must come from the resume or the user's messages. Never invent employers, titles, numbers, certifications, or tools.
- Keep the user's numbers exactly as given. If a number is missing, write the sentence without one and ask for it — do not estimate.
- Mark inferred labels (for example "Senior") with [confirm].

## Headline (LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters)

Pattern: role or target role | specialty or domain | one proof point or audience.

- Lead with the words a recruiter would search for the target role.
- One proof point beats a list of adjectives.
- Avoid: "passionate", "guru", "ninja", "results-driven", emoji strings, "open to work" (LinkedIn has a separate feature for that).

## About

Three short paragraphs, first person, under ~1,500 characters (LinkedIn allows 2,600):

1. What you do and for whom, in one or two sentences.
2. Two or three concrete results from the resume, with the user's numbers.
3. What you are looking for next and how to reach you (if the user wants that public).

End with 5–8 skills the user actually listed, as plain words, not hashtags.
