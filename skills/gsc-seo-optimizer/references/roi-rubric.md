# Opportunity rubric

Use qualitative prioritization unless the user supplied conversion and cost data. Search impressions alone are not revenue potential.

| Candidate | Evidence needed | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant query near positions 4–20 | Page-specific non-brand query, sufficient impressions for the site's scale, matching product intent | Inspect snippet, content gaps and contextual internal links |
| Low CTR at a useful rank | Relevant query/device context; brand sitelinks and tiny samples excluded | Inspect actual SERP and snippet before rewriting |
| Relevant query beyond position 20 | Clear business value and a credible competitive/content gap | Larger test with lower near-term confidence, not a quick title win |
| Possible cannibalization | Same intent, overlapping pages, unstable preferred URL and evidence of harm | Choose a primary page only after checking content and links |
| Confirmed technical barrier | Inspection or rendered evidence of noindex/canonical/fetch problem | Fix the technical cause within approved scope |
| Newly optimized or weak evidence | Too few complete days, sparse queries, unrelated intent | Observe; no action is a valid recommendation |

Tie each action to a specific observation. Do not use a universal CTR benchmark as proof, a KD score as proof of easy ranking, or a high impression count as proof of product fit.

Prioritize a coherent small batch by business relevance, evidence strength, likely effort, and current visibility. Explain uncertainty and skip reasons. Review CTR after an adequate comparable window; ranking changes may require several weeks or longer, especially at low volume.
